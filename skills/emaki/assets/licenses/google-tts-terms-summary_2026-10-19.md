# Google Cloud Text-to-Speech (Chirp 3 HD): terms that apply to a generated voice-over

Read in full on 2026-10-19. This is our own summary, not legal advice, with section numbers, not the
binding text. The binding text is always the current one on Google's site:

- Google Cloud Platform Terms of Service, last modified October 12, 2026: https://cloud.google.com/terms
- Service Specific Terms, last modified October 12, 2026, sections 14-20 (AI/ML Services): https://cloud.google.com/terms/service-terms
- Services Summary, last modified October 12, 2026: https://cloud.google.com/terms/services
- Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy, last modified December 17, 2024: https://policies.google.com/terms/generative-ai/use-policy
- Acceptable Use Policy, last modified October 12, 2026: https://cloud.google.com/terms/aup
- Generative AI Indemnified Services, last modified July 20, 2026: https://cloud.google.com/terms/generative-ai-indemnified-services
- Chirp 3: HD voices documentation: https://docs.cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/docs/chirp3-hd

## Which terms apply

- Text-to-Speech is listed under AI/ML Services, Pre-Trained APIs (Services Summary).
- The Chirp 3 HD documentation says the voices are powered by Google's latest generative models.
  The Services Summary says Generative AI Services also include any generative AI feature of a
  Service. So the voice-over falls under Service Specific Terms section 20 (Generative AI
  Services) as well as the general AI/ML sections 14-19.
- Chirp 3 HD is generally available for en-US and uk-UA (only Punjabi and Chinese (Hong Kong)
  are in Preview), so no Pre-GA terms apply.

## Who owns the audio

- Service Specific Terms 20(a): the content a Generative AI Service generates from the
  customer's input is "Generated Output", Generated Output is Customer Data, and Google does not
  assert ownership of any new intellectual property created in it.
- Terms of Service, definitions: Customer Data includes the data the customer derives from its
  own data through the Services. Section 5.1: the customer keeps all intellectual property
  rights in Customer Data.
- Result: as between Google and the account holder, the voice-over audio belongs to the account
  holder. No document limits its use in a commercial film, an ad or a client's video. No credit
  or attribution to Google is required.

## What we must not do with it

1. Use a generated voice as part of a website, app or online service directed at, or likely to
   be used by, people under 18 (Service Specific Terms 20(d)). For this skill: no generated voice
   in a film made for children or teenagers.
2. Pass the voice off as a real person, or claim a human recorded it in order to deceive;
   impersonate a real person (Prohibited Use Policy, misrepresentation).
3. Use the Services to send unsolicited mass email, promotions or ads (AUP, spam). A film with
   the voice in a cold mass mailing breaks this; a film sent to people who expect it does not.
4. Use the audio to build or train another voice model, or to develop a competing product
   (Service Specific Terms 17(a), 17(b)).
5. The usual content rules: nothing illegal, hateful, harassing, sexually explicit, fraudulent,
   or infringing others' rights (Prohibited Use Policy, AUP).

## What the client should know

- The voice is a library voice. Other companies can use the same voice, and Google says a
  Generative AI Service may produce the same or similar output for several customers
  (Service Specific Terms 20(b)). The voice is not exclusive to the client's brand.
- Google gives no indemnity for this voice: Text-to-Speech is not on the Generative AI
  Indemnified Services list, and that indemnity never covers use provided free of charge
  (Service Specific Terms 20(i)). If a third party ever claimed rights in the audio, Google would
  not defend it.
